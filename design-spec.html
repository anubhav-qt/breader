breader

A reader for the web. Open a book in your browser, read it like a book or like a web page, and come back to the exact sentence where you stopped.

spec v0.2draft25 sep 2026desktop web
Since v0.1
  • Web pages only. Every screen is drawn in a browser window at desktop size. The phone and Mac app frames are gone.
  • Our own look. Doto for the interface, Dongle for reading. Flat surfaces and hairlines instead of glass, and no iOS-style controls.
  • A left nav for reading. A slim rail that expands into Contents and Appearance. It replaces the floating pill.
  • Five library options, all live on this page. Pick one or combine them.
  • Motion as springs. Every animation uses one of three springs, set by response and damping.

Library options

Five ways to show your books.

The library is the first page you see, so it gets the most thought. Every option below is live. They all open a book the same way: the page grows out of what you clicked and lands on the sentence where you stopped.

1

Last lines

Each book appears as the sentence you stopped at. The newest is large and the rest step down. Unstarted books show their first line, finished books their last.

Reader and nav

A rail that stays out of the way.

While you read, the only interface is a 64 px rail on the left. It shows where you are in the whole book and holds three buttons. Contents and Appearance expand it into a panel, and the page slides aside without reflowing.

Open Contents and Appearance, switch Book and Modern, change the theme or type, and click the page edges or use ← → to turn pages

Adding books

Private with a key, or shared with everyone.

Whoever adds a book decides who can read it. Private books need no account: the first private upload creates a library key, and that key opens the same books in any browser. Dragging a file anywhere onto the window starts the flow.

Motion

Three springs, and nothing else.

Like Apple’s interfaces, motion is physical: each animation is a spring set by response (how quickly it arrives) and damping (how much it overshoots), never a fixed duration. The curves below are the real ones used on this page.

Text never bounces
Anything that holds reading text moves with Smooth. Overshoot is only for small objects you touch directly.
Continuity
What opens from something closes back into it: a book grows out of its line, ribbon or card.
Interruptible
A new click redirects a moving element from wherever it is. Nothing makes you wait for it to finish.
Reduced motion
When the system asks for less motion, everything becomes a 150 ms fade.

Type and colour

Doto to steer, Dongle to read.

Two faces with clear jobs. Doto, a dot-matrix face, is for the interface: labels, buttons, numbers. Dongle, soft and rounded, is for anything you read. Both are set to the same x-height, so they sit level on a line.

Unchanged from v0.1.

Your place is saved as chapter, paragraph and character, never as a page number. After any change of size, typeface, layout or window, Breader opens the page that holds that character. Percent and time left come from word counts, at 230 words a minute.

Open questions

Decisions for the next round.

Which library option

Pick one, or combine: for example Last lines as the home page, with the Dial or Ribbons as a second view.

Doto at small sizes

Dot-matrix type gets hard to read below about 13 px, so labels stay at 13 px or larger, bold and short.

Accept fewer, larger labels. It suits the minimal direction anyway.

Dongle as the only reading face

The spec makes Dongle the default for both Book and Modern. Every other face, starting with Oxanium, is a choice in Appearance’s typeface list.

Where Breader lives

Keys and the shared shelf need server storage, which a public Claude artifact can’t have.

Host it ourselves with file storage and a key index.

What Immersive means

The slot is reserved. It’s listed in TODO.md along with phone-width layouts and the keyboard-first mode.
